MODEL & IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

What this simulator computes

This is an interactive research simulator inspired by PEQA, not a claim of exact reproduction of the paper’s classroom experiment. The equations below describe the implemented baseline and make its simplifications explicit.

01

Generate true scores and reports

Blank true scores are sampled from a clipped Normal with mean μ and variance 1/γ, or uniformly over the configured score range. Blank grader biases and reliabilities are sampled from the chosen distributions. Each assigned report is

ỹⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾ = yⱼ + bᵢ + εᵢⱼ,   εᵢⱼ ∼ N(0, 1/τᵢ)

Noise draws are independent across grader–paper pairs. Manual values override random generation row by row.

02

Estimate from probe papers

For each grader, bias is estimated by the mean difference between reports and instructor scores on that grader’s probes.

b̂ᵢ = mean(ỹⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾ − yⱼ)

Estimated reliability uses the paper’s residual sum-of-squares estimator:

τ̂ᵢ = (|Pᵢ| − 1) / Σⱼ∈Pᵢ (ỹⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾ − yⱼ − b̂ᵢ)²

For numerical stability, a near-zero residual sum is capped at τ̂=1,000,000.

03

Aggregate non-probe grades

PEQA de-biases reports and weights each one by √τ̂ᵢ, with prior weight √γ.

rⱼ = [√γ μ + Σᵢ √τ̂ᵢ(ỹⱼ⁽ⁱ⁾−b̂ᵢ)] / [√γ + Σᵢ √τ̂ᵢ]

ERM uses τ̂ᵢ and prior weight γ. Median and Mean aggregate raw reports. Probe papers receive the instructor-verified score for every method.

04

Measure accuracy and regrading

RMSE and MAE are calculated over non-probe papers using their assigned score before regrading. The assumed student final score is

gⱼ = max(rⱼ, yⱼ)

Grader performance reward is α times the difference between negative squared error with and without that grader. In this simulated environment, true scores are known to measure that reward.

Assumptions, constraints & interpretation

  • The assignment is constrained: each grader receives K/2 probes and K/2 non-probes; each non-probe paper receives K/2 or K/2+1 graders; self-grading is prohibited.
  • Probe status is hidden from simulated graders when reports are generated. True-score bounds and the optional clamping toggle create bounded-score variants that depart from the paper’s unbounded theoretical model.
  • The paper’s Theorem 1 assumes each grader’s total other-regarding weight is at most one. With a uniform competitive weight, this condition is w(n−1) ≤ 1; the simulator reports the selected values but does not force this condition.
  • The “Precision incentives” tab is a finite Monte Carlo utility sweep for one grader. It is diagnostic and can contain sampling noise. It does not implement the paper’s complete costly-effort mechanism and Theorem 3 modified score π.
  • Results are synthetic unless you manually enter a real dataset. They do not reproduce the authors’ classroom data or establish statistical significance.
